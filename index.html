    Pathlight Project

    Machine Learning and Backend Engineer

    San Francisco, California · May to August 2026

    Pathlight builds PATH, a voice-first assistant for blind and low-vision people. You speak naturally, and it reads, summarizes, searches, drafts and replies to email and manages your calendar across Gmail, Outlook, Google Calendar and iCloud Mail, without navigating every screen by hand.

    The company was founded by Ariyan Patel of Yale and Kushal Patel and Ronak Saramya of Stanford. It began as a wearable navigation device and grew into software, and its features are co-designed and tested with blind and low-vision users.

    I spent the summer with the team in the Yale Hacker House in San Francisco. My main work was the harness behind the assistant: the system that takes what someone says, works out the task, and carries it through with low latency and high accuracy.

    I worked on the system's architecture and fine-tuned language models to improve their performance, efficiency and accuracy.

    Accuracy matters more when you can't check the screen. Before PATH sends or changes anything important, it reads back the key details, and when a request is unclear it asks instead of guessing.

    I also built UI features for both the mobile and desktop apps, designed around a blind and low-vision audience.

    The company is now in the process of being sold. pathlightproj.com
